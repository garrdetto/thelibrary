Fairway Nine

Nine original holes where every shot is a projectile problem: launch angle, spin, air drag and wind decide where the ball lands, then the slope and surface decide where it stops.

How the swing and the physics work
  • Power: hold the button (click, Space or touch) and let go when the outer ring reaches the white marker. The faded balls show where a perfect strike at that marker goes; R/F moves the marker.
  • Path: the cursor runs down to the bottom mark; tap as it gets there. Early pulls left, late pushes right.
  • Contact: it races back to the top; click on the top mark and keep holding. Early closes the face (hook), late opens it (slice).
  • Rhythm: an inner ring grows from the centre; release as it meets its mark. Letting go early or late, or tapping instead of holding, costs up to 7 % of the distance.
  • Near the green pick a chip (low, runs out), a flop (high, lands soft) or a spinner (low, checks hard and can zip back) with 1–4 or T.
  • Launch speed is power × club speed × lie: the rough costs 20–40 %, a bunker more than half.
  • Drag grows with the square of speed; backspin creates lift (the Magnus effect) and a steeper landing; wind pushes hardest high in the air.
  • On the green the slope pulls the ball downhill, so read the break before you putt.
