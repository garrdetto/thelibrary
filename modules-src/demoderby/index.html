How the physics and the rules work
  • Damage follows the impact force: momentum (mass × speed) lost in a split second. Hit harder, hurt more; a full-strength hit takes about a third of a car.
  • The nose carries the engine. Front hits cost power and hole the radiator, which cooks the engine under throttle until it smokes, steams and can catch fire. Backing into someone protects your motor.
  • Every corner breaks on its own: bent wheels lose grip and pull the steering, tires blow, wheels come off and drag the car, a bent rear end saps drive, bumpers sag and drop, lamps and glass shatter. The diagram by your gauges shows it all.
  • The frame bends on hard T-bones, so the car crabs sideways and drags. Crushed fenders rub and shred their tires, big nose hits can stall the engine for a moment, a battered tail can strip forward or reverse gear (you keep the other one), hoods and trunk lids buckle then tear off, and a sagging exhaust drags sparks until it falls away.
  • Barriers bite too, a little less than a car. Damage level (Light, Realistic, Brutal) sets how hard every hit lands.
  • Dirt gives less sideways grip than pavement, so the tail slides on hard turns. Torque is strongest off the line, so short, straight runs land the heaviest hits.
  • A car that goes 60 seconds without striking another one is called out of the heat, just like a fair derby.
  • Points: hits landed, knockouts, surviving seconds and 1000 for the win. The best heats lead the board.
