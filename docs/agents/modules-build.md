# Learning module build pipeline and Sandlot Sluggers

How module pages and models are built, plus the Sandlot Sluggers modules. Moved out of AGENTS.md so it loads only when this area is worked on; keep it current here, not in AGENTS.md.

- Learning-module pages are built from `modules-src/<id>/` (`index.html` + `index.ts`, plus `main.js` for the older single-file modules) into `public/modules/<id>.html` and hashed chunks in `public/modules/chunks/` by `vite.modules.ts`, which `vite.config.ts` runs as part of `pnpm build` and `pnpm dev`. The generated files are git-ignored. Exported models live in `assets-src/models/` and `scripts/assets.mjs` (run by `pnpm assets`, and by `vite.assets.ts` before every build and `pnpm dev`) writes the optimized `public/models/` (meshopt, WebP, joined by material; see `docs/ENGINE-MAP.md` and `docs/ASSETS.md`). The outputs are committed so deploys never run the pipeline: after changing anything in `assets-src/models/`, run `pnpm assets` and commit `public/models/` with it (CI's `pnpm assets:check` fails otherwise); never hand-edit files under `public/models/`. three.js (0.186.1), PlayCanvas (2.23.3, Northwoods Angler and Kestrel County), Babylon.js (9.34.0 with `@babylonjs/loaders`, Rack & Reel, Life Sim, Tello, Thrill Park 2 and Short Track next), Rapier, PeerJS, colyseus.js and cannon-es are pinned in `package.json` and bundled; never load them from a CDN. `docs/ENGINE-MAP.md` lists every file. Built modules: Sandlot Sluggers 3D (`sandlot3d`), Sandlot Sluggers (`sandlot`), BotWreck 3D (`botwreck`), chess, checkers, Short Track Showdown (`shorttrack`), Fox Plains (`maplestreet`), Life Sim (`foxlake`), Fairway Nine (`fairway`), Demo Derby (`demoderby`), Pro Skater AI (`proskater`) and Northwoods Angler (`angler`, PlayCanvas instead of the shared three.js engine); 2048 and Chopper Cave stay as plain files in `public/modules/`. All are embedded in an iframe at `#module-<id>`. Sandlot Sluggers 3D (`modules-src/sandlot3d/main.js`) is a classic script that reads a global `THREE` provided by `modules-src/sandlot3d/three-legacy.ts` (r128-compatible color and lighting over the bundled `three/webgpu`, plus `THREE.TSL`). It renders with `WebGPURenderer` (WebGPU, WebGL2 backend as the fallback or with `?renderer=webgl`), so its materials must be node materials or the standard ones; write custom shading in TSL, never GLSL `ShaderMaterial` or `onBeforeCompile`. The field's turf and clay detail comes from `modules-src/sandlot3d/tools/build-field.py` (Blender; outputs `assets-src/models/sandlot3d/field_detail.glb`, then `pnpm assets`). The module loads its roster/batter-card art from `public/modules/sprites/`. Its 3D players (`class Rig`) are built as 6-ft athletes about 7.5 heads tall (rig units times `RIG_S`), then squashed to 75% height by `RIG_H` (the head is scaled back up so it stays round; the strike zone `ZC`/`ZH` matches the shorter batters). The body is a skinned model built in Blender by `modules-src/sandlot3d/tools/build-athlete.py` (run it in Blender, then `pnpm assets`; it writes `assets-src/models/sandlot3d/athlete.glb`), loaded by `index.ts` and posed every frame from the same pose and IK joints; its material slots are named for the uniform channels below. If the model can't load (or with `?athlete=0`) the older procedural body is used: a sculpted chest, pelvis and abdomen, and each arm and leg one continuous `LimbTube` (`armProfile`, `legProfile`). Head, hands, fielder's glove and cleats are separate procedural meshes either way. On the field every player is their draft character: the card art (`public/modules/sprites/pNN.webp`) made into a Meshy model and fitted to the same skeleton by `modules-src/sandlot3d/tools/build-toons.py` (outputs `assets-src/models/sandlot3d/toons/pNN.glb`, then `pnpm assets`), loaded per player by `window.__toon` and swapped in by `Rig.useToon`; `?toon=0` shows the uniformed athletes below. Customisation channels: `PLAYER_LOOK[i]` (skin, skinMap, hair, hairStyle short/buzz/long/curly/dreads/bald, facialHair none/stubble/mustache/goatee/beard, eyes, number, sleeves, build) and `UNIFORMS.you` / `UNIFORMS.cpu` (jersey, trim, letters, undershirt, pants, belt, socks, stirrups, cleats, cap, bill, helmet); at runtime `rig.setLook({...})` and `setUniform(team,{...})`, both also on `window.__ss`. Batters and runners wear helmets, fielders caps. Sculpted faces and PBR materials (`STD`, `SKIN`, `HAIR`, PMREM sky reflection) are unchanged; roster art is WebP in `public/modules/sprites/`. Field geometry is in world feet with the back point of home plate at the origin (the foul lines run from it); the pitch crosses the zone (`ZZ`, `ZC`, `ZH`) over the middle of the plate. When the user's team fields, the defense never throws on its own: the player clicks a base marker (or presses 1–4) and `userThrow` sends the ball. The HUD shows a ghost ball where the last pitch crossed and a compact release meter beside the zone. The 2D Sandlot Sluggers (`modules-src/sandlot/index.html`) draws its kids from Blender-rendered atlases in `public/modules/sprites/kids/` made by `modules-src/sandlot/tools/build-kids.py` from the game's own `ROSTER`; after changing a kid's look, costume or a pose in `poseFor`, re-run it for that kid (`blender -b -P modules-src/sandlot/tools/build-kids.py -- <id>`) and commit the atlas and `kids.json`; the close-up batter comes from `modules-src/sandlot/tools/build-batter.py` the same way (`bat-<id>.webp`, `batters.json`), so re-run it after changing a kid or `SWING3`/`BUNT3`/`drawBatter3D`. Online play in these uses PeerJS room codes, not the `server/` service.
