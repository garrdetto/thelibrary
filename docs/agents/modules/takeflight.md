# Take Flight

Career flight simulator (`modules-src/takeflight/`, module id `takeflight`, page `public/modules/takeflight.html`, thumbnail `public/images/modules/takeflight.webp`). Babylon.js 9.34 on WebGPU with the WebGL2 fallback (Rack & Reel's `createEngine`), reverse depth buffer, right-handed scene. Design, milestones and architecture decisions: `docs/takeflight/PLAN.md`. Files: `docs/ENGINE-MAP.md` under takeflight. Keep this file current; never AGENTS.md.

## Scoring, debrief and replays

- `sim/Telemetry.ts` (`FlightRecorder`) listens to every tick: the 12-byte input log, 24 Float32 telemetry channels at 10 Hz (`CH`), and touchdown events at 120 Hz (a touchdown needs 0.2 s airborne before it; its G is the 0.1 s low-passed load factor, not the gear spring's raw spikes).
- `sim/Scoring.ts` (`scoreFlight`) is pure and exact. Categories and weights: landing 40, approach 20, smoothness 15, procedure 15, operations 10; each metric scores 0..1 with `band(value, good, bad)`. The landing is the last touchdown after 8 s or more airborne; shorter hops within 10 s after it are bounces. Unflown categories score zero and the grade is Incomplete; a crash scores 0. The assists multiplier applies to the total. Change thresholds there and keep `tests/scoring.test.ts` passing.
- `sim/Starts.ts` places every start with `SimMath` only; `sim/Reflight.ts` re-flies a record (start or snapshot plus input log). `Simulation.saveState/loadState` (full `FlightModel` state including gear contacts, plus wind) must stay complete: `tests/determinism.test.ts` continues from a snapshot bit for bit. Add any new state that survives between ticks to `FlightModel.saveState`.
- `game/FlightSession.ts` takes a snapshot every 5 s, streams finished minutes to `game/record.worker.ts`, toasts each touchdown, and ends the flight 2.5 s after the aircraft stops on its wheels following a landing (also Pause → End flight, or 2 s after a crash). `game/TouchdownReplay.ts` re-flies 10 s before to 6 s after the touchdown from the nearest snapshot; the camera sits beside the runway (the Camera button swaps to chase; Escape returns to the debrief).
- Flight records (`shared/FlightRecord.ts`): `TFR1` + raw DEFLATE of framed JSON header, XOR-delta inputs, delta-coded byte-plane telemetry and JSON footer. Bump `RECORD_VERSION` when the layout or anything that changes a re-flown result changes.
- The sim costs about 20 µs a tick in Node (bundled, unminified), so a replay never re-flies from the start of a long flight; milestone 7 has to measure the Worker's budget against that.

## The region

- `shared/Airports.ts`: Harrow Field (hand laid out) plus six airports from `layout()`: Millbrook Strip (grass 14/32), Ashby Regional (15/33, ILS), Fenwick Freight Field (cargo, 04/22, ILS), Corran County (14/32, ILS), Halvard International (hub, parallel 12L/30R and 12R/30L, ILS) and Crag Notch (mountain field at 1,065 m, land on 06 only: `landingEnds`). Sites were picked by searching the seeded terrain for clear approaches; `tests/airports.test.ts` keeps every airport flat, every landing end clear below a 2° slope (3.5° at Crag Notch) out to 5 km (10 km with ILS), airports apart, and every start valid. Moving an airport means re-running that search: the natural ground decides where approaches are clear.
- Starts are generic per airport (`runway1`/`runway2`/`final1`/`final2` on `runways[0]`, `stand`, `cruise` 9 km out at least 450 m above the highest ground on the way in). Scoring finds the landing runway at whichever airport the wheels touch (`nearestAirport`).
- `shared/Approach.ts` (`approachFor`) gives glideslope and localizer dots for the landing runway end the aircraft is lined up with; ILS ends drive the HUD needles (an instrument, always on), any end drives the landing-guidance bar.
- `game/World.ts` is the floating origin: terrain and airports hang under `world.root` at minus the origin; the aircraft, camera and tower are drawn at world minus origin; when the aircraft strays 3 km the origin jumps to the kilometre under it and frozen world matrices and bounding boxes under the root are recomputed. Anything new that is placed in world metres goes under `world.root` (and through `world.refresh` if it is frozen before the next rebase) or is converted with the origin. Airports build within 26 km of the camera and are released beyond 34 km. Airport geometry is still in absolute metres (static millimetre rounding far from Harrow); moving it to airport-relative coordinates is milestone 8 polish.

## Weather

- `shared/Weather.ts`: presets clear, fair, overcast, rain, storm, fog (a ground layer) and changing (a seeded 36-minute cycle through fair, overcast and rain with the wind veering and strengthening). `weatherAt` gives cover, base and depth above the departure field, visibility, rain, and gust/turbulence/veer/speed factors; `weatherWind` applies them to the chosen base wind. The sim owns it (`Simulation.weather`, updated once a sim second for changing weather, recomputed on `loadState`), so the wind it stirs replays exactly; the record header carries `weather` (record version 3). Careers multiply XP by `WEATHER_XP`; the Instrument checkride is flown in fog.
- `render/WeatherView.ts` draws it: `render/Clouds.ts` ray-marches a 3D value-noise slab on two planes at the base and top (only the plane between camera and layer draws, so nothing counts twice and terrain occludes through depth), GLSL and WGSL kept in step, hidden on a compile error; inside a thick layer a camera-centred bubble whites out the view (planes cannot reach the horizon); `render/Rain.ts` thin-instanced streaks below the base; visibility through `setFogRange`; sunlight dimmed by cover; the clock runs on during the flight. Change the two shader copies together.

## Towns and the city

- `shared/Towns.ts`: `PLACES` (Halvard city beside the hub, Ashby and Corran towns beside their airports, Fenwick Works industrial district, Harrow and Millbrook villages), each a rotated street grid filled from a seed: towers and offices downtown in the city, shops on the main street, houses toward the edge, warehouses in the works. Buildings stay off airport ground, water and slopes over 22 %, and below `heightLimit` (every runway's approach and departure surface: 2.5° from the runway end, 150 m either side widening 15 %), so approaches pass over or beside them. `tests/towns.test.ts` checks all of it.
- Buildings are obstacles: `FlightModel.step` crashes the aircraft (`impact`) when it is within 260 m of the ground and below `obstacleTop` (exact rotated footprints found through a 64 m grid). That is part of the simulation, so the Worker's re-flight sees the same buildings; places are laid out once, on the loading screen.
- `render/TownRenderer.ts` draws a place with thin-instanced boxes per kind (colour per instance), gabled roofs, window grids that light at night on offices and towers, and draped streets; houses, shops and streets only within 7 km. `game/World.ts` builds places within 22 km of their edge and releases them beyond 28 km. Trees are not planted in built-up areas (`townWeight`).

## Terrain

- `render/TerrainTile.ts` builds a tile's vertex data (pure; heights, normals, colours, UVs, skirt, and `tfMorph`, each vertex's height on the parent's twice-as-coarse grid). `render/terrain.worker.ts` runs it off the main thread; the renderer builds synchronously only while loading (`update(..., Infinity)`) or where workers are missing (tests). At most 8 tiles are in flight.
- `render/TerrainMorph.ts` is a material plugin on the terrain material (GLSL and WGSL): the vertex height blends toward `tfMorph` from 62 % to 94 % of the tile's hand-over distance (2 × size × `terrainSplit`, where the parent stops splitting), so a tile matches its parent when they swap. The eye is passed in the tile's own coordinates. A compile error turns the plugin off (`disable()`) rather than losing the terrain.

## Fleet

- `shared/Fleet.ts` (page and tests) parses every aircraft JSON; `shared/Hangar.ts` (Worker) lists id, family, category and the licence needed to fly it outside a checkride. Add both when adding an aircraft, plus its models and a row in `tests/fleet.test.ts` (rest on the wheels with no structure touching, ground roll, climb with gear up, full-power level speed, stall near Vs1).
- New aircraft are built by `tools/blender/build_fleet.py` from parameter dicts (fuselage sections with a top exponent, wing, tail or T-tail, nose prop / wing nacelles / rear-fuselage fans, retractable tricycle gear, doors, a generic cockpit with `panel_screen`): `python wrapper build_fleet.py -- assets-src/models/takeflight [name ...]`. Contacts in the JSON use body axes (x fwd, y up, z right) = Blender (x, z, -y), at the tyre bottoms and prop tips.
- `Simulation.setAircraft` swaps the flight model between flights; `Standalone.useAircraft` swaps the visual and cockpit. Flying an aircraft needs its hangar licence (checkrides bring their own aircraft). The record header's `aircraft` picks the spec for replays.
- Hangar licences: Wren Student, Duet Private Pilot, Sprite Instrument Rating, Vesta Commercial Pilot. The ATP checkride flies the Vesta.
- Jets: turbofan thrust lapses with density and Mach (`1 − 0.55 M + 0.2 M²`), and every aircraft has a transonic drag rise (`aero.machCrit`, default 0.72, and `machDrag`). Fleet cockpits (`build_fleet.py`) end the interior shell open just ahead of the panel so nothing fills the windshield.
- Retractable gear (`controls.retractable`, `gearTime`) folds in `AircraftVisual`: mains forward, nose aft, hidden when stowed. Contacts marked `retracts` stop touching when up.
- Engine failures: `FlightSetup.failure` (`{ engine, agl }`) fails that engine once the aircraft climbs through `agl` after take-off; `FlightModel.failedEngine` is part of the snapshot, the dead engine gives windmill drag and its thrust moment goes, and the record header carries it. The multi-engine checkride flies the Duet with engine 2 failing at 120 m and needs `engineFailure` in the summary.

## Cargo career

- `shared/Cargo.ts`: eight contracts a day (UTC), the same for everyone, generated from the day number (`jobsFor`): origin, destination, goods, kg and pay (distance and weight). A contract stays open the day it is offered and the next (`openJob`). `cargoPay` pays 60–100 % of the contract with the score, only for landing on a runway at the destination without a crash.
- Liveries: `shared/Liveries.ts` lists paint schemes per aircraft family (the first is the factory finish). The start screen's Paint choice saves one per family in this browser (`takeflight.liveries.v1`, a convenience, not career data); `AircraftVisual.applyLivery` recolours every LOD's `<family>_paint`, `_stripe` and `_cowl` materials.
- Window openings are picked in the fuselage loft's own parameters (`aircraft_kit.loft_faces` and `window_picks`: x between sections, angle around the ring, 0 = left side, pi/2 = top), so the outer glass and the cockpit shell's openings share the same edges, which follow mesh lines. Specs: `WINDOWS` in `build_wren.py`, `windows` in each `build_fleet.py` parameter set ('front', 'side' with 'posts', 'roof').
- Careers keep `cash` and `cargo` (deliveries, kg). `applyFlight` pays a contract from its id (the Worker regenerates it), refuses loads heavier than the aircraft's `HANGAR` payload (max take-off − empty − default fuel; `tests/career.test.ts` checks it against the JSON) and adds `kg / 20` XP.
- The Cargo contracts screen (`CareerPanel.showCargo`) offers each contract in the lightest aircraft the pilot may fly that lifts it; the flight starts on the origin's stand with the load as payload (`FlightSetup.payload`, record header `payload` and `job`). The debrief shows the pay or why it was not paid.

## Careers

- `shared/Career.ts` holds every rule and runs on both sides: `applyFlight(profile, summary, context)` returns the new profile, XP, level change, Pilot Rating change, checkride result and any licence. XP = total × 2 × duration (0.5 + minutes/10, at most 3) × wind (calm 1 → gusty 1.5) × night 1.2, plus a landing bonus; nothing for a crash or under 30 s; at most 2,500 a flight. Level n needs `120 × (n − 1)^1.7` XP (level 100 at about 296,000). The Pilot Rating is the 0.85-decay weighted mean of the last 20 totals per class (three needed), and overall weights classes by hours.
- Licences: Student → Private → Instrument → Multi-engine → Commercial → Airline Transport. Each `CHECKRIDE_SPECS` entry lists the licences it needs, a level, hours, its class, the flight setup and pass criteria read from the `FlightSummary` (category scores, `category.metric` scores, landing facts, assists, `instrumentsOnly`). Checkrides whose class has no aircraft in `shared/Hangar.ts` stay locked (multi-engine and ATP until a twin and a jet arrive). A checkride flown before the pilot qualifies is recorded as a failed attempt.
- `shared/Hangar.ts` lists each aircraft's id, family and category for the Worker; `tests/career.test.ts` checks it against the JSON. Add every new aircraft there.
- Saves: signed in, `/api/takeflight` (`src/server/routes/takeflight.ts`, D1 `takeflight_careers`, migration 0050, table also created on first use) recomputes each flight with `applyFlight`, rejects a flight longer than the time since the last save, and saves only if nobody saved in between. Guests keep the career in browser storage (`takeflight.career.v1`, `game/CareerStore.ts`), as the plan allows. A resumed flight (Keep flying) submits only its extra seconds and replaces its own rating entry.
- Scores are still the page's numbers; milestone 7 re-flies submitted records on the Worker before leaderboards trust them.

## Rules that matter

- **Determinism.** Everything under `sim/` and `shared/` is renderer-free and uses only `sim/SimMath.ts` (`sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `exp`, `ln`, `pow`, ...) plus `+ - * /`, `Math.sqrt`, `Math.floor`, `Math.round`, `Math.abs`, `Math.imul`. Never `Math.sin`/`Math.pow`/`Math.hypot`/`**` in sim code: engines may differ in the last bit, and the Worker re-flies recorded flights to check scores. The only randomness is `SimRandom` seeded per flight. `tests/determinism.test.ts` replays a recorded input log and requires bit-identical state.
- **Fixed step.** `sim/Simulation.ts` runs the model at 120 Hz (`SIM_DT`); the renderer interpolates between the last two ticks. Inputs are quantised to bytes (`sim/Controls.ts`) before the model sees them, so a 12-byte-per-tick log replays exactly.
- **Axes.** World x east, y up, z south (heading 0 = north = -z). Body x forward, y up, z right. Positive inputs: pitch = back stick (nose up), roll = right, yaw = right pedal. Control-surface hinge rotations in `render/AircraftVisual.ts`: about body +z a positive angle moves the trailing edge down.
- **Aircraft data** is JSON checked by `shared/AircraftSpec.ts` (Zod). Coefficients are per unit input in the conventions above. After changing aero numbers, run `node scripts/takeflight-tests.mjs flight` (envelope: roll 180–330 m, Vy climb 450–850 fpm, full-power level 104–128 kt, stall near Vs1, trimmed 3° approach).
- **Models.** Aircraft are built by Blender Python: `blender -b --factory-startup -P modules-src/takeflight/tools/blender/build_wren.py -- assets-src/models/takeflight [preview.png]` (Blender 5.2 at `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2`). Without Blender (cloud sessions), `pip install bpy==5.2.4` in a virtualenv and run the script through a wrapper that does `import bpy` before `runpy.run_path(script)` with `sys.argv = [script, '--', out_dir]` (`bmesh` only imports after `bpy`); previews need `libEGL`, so check the model in the page instead. The Wren's wing panels run in to the centreline and the cabin's upper section has square shoulders (the sixth `FUSELAGE` value is the top exponent) so the high wing sits on the roof with no gap. `tools/blender/export_aircraft.py` validates names, scale, size, triangle budgets per LOD, materials, textures and UV overlaps, and fails the build on any violation; it writes `<name>_lod0..2.glb` and `<name>_cockpit.glb`. Then `node scripts/assets.mjs` (the `takeflight/` preset keeps every node, empty `light_*` nodes and UVs). Author with nose +X, left wing +Y, up +Z in Blender; that exports straight onto body axes. Materials need backface culling on (the cockpit view relies on the outer fuselage being invisible from inside).
- **Reused code.** Sky, sun, fog, shadows and the HDR post chain are Truck Simulator's (`trucksim/render/world/Atmosphere.ts`, `Pipeline.ts`, `render/Quality.ts` tier names); `Scope` is `trucksim/systems/Scope.ts`; the engine boot and resolution control are Rack & Reel's. Changes there affect Take Flight.
- **Faces.** Hand-built meshes (terrain tiles, runway quads) wind so the right-handed cross product of (b-a)×(c-a) points down; the other way they are culled. Copy the existing index order.
- **Cockpit panel.** `render/Instruments.ts` paints the gauges into a dynamic texture on `panel_screen`; the texture is flipped with `vScale = -1` and needs `WRAP` addressing.
- No visible copy says game; every maker, carrier and airline is invented.

## Local testing

The in-app preview often has no free server slots; use the light Vite config and headless Edge:

- Server: `node node_modules/vite/bin/vite.js --config node_modules/.cache/vite.takeflight.config.mjs --port 3091` in the worktree (root `modules-src/takeflight`, public dir `public/`).
- Shots: `Q="?quality=medium" STEPS='[{"eval":"window.__takeflight.debug.fly({start:\"final09\"})"},{"wait":4000,"shot":"final"}]' node node_modules/.cache/tf-shot.mjs C:/dev/tf` (Playwright, Edge, WebGPU). `window.__takeflight.debug` has `fly(options)`, `view(mode)`, `end()` (debrief now), `replay()`, the `sim` and the `session`. To reach a debrief with a landing, pause the sim, drive `sim.pilot` and `sim.stepPilot()` from a script (the scripted pilot in `tests/helpers.ts` is a template), the frame loop opens the debrief once the aircraft has stood still for 2.5 s.
- `?perf=1` or the backquote key opens the performance overlay.
- Tests: `node scripts/takeflight-tests.mjs [filter]` (esbuild bundle, `node --test`), also in `pnpm test` after `tsc -p modules-src/takeflight/tsconfig.json`.
